# Purpose

This skill defines how to design landing pages for high-ticket offers: premium services, consultancies, large projects, and expensive products.

High-ticket decisions require more trust, more information, and more risk reduction than low-commitment offers.

---

# Scope

Applies to offers with significant price points or commitment levels: consultancies, premium services, large custom projects, high-value products.

# High-Ticket Visitor Psychology

High-ticket buyers ask, beyond standard questions:

- "Is this worth the investment?"
- "What exactly is the process?"
- "What could go wrong, and how is that handled?"
- "Who am I actually working with?"
- "Do they understand my specific situation?"

---

# Priority Elements

## Authority and Expertise

Establish credibility clearly: experience, credentials, notable results, media mentions, or recognized expertise in the field.

---

## Process Transparency

High-ticket buyers want to understand what they're committing to. A clear "how it works" or process section significantly reduces anxiety.

---

## Detailed Case Studies

Prefer in-depth case studies over short testimonials — show the specific problem, approach, and measurable outcome for a client in a similar situation.

---

## Value Framing

Justify the price through the value/outcome delivered, not through comparison to cheaper alternatives. Frame cost against the cost of inaction or of solving the problem elsewhere/poorly.

---

## Personalized Qualification

Rather than a simple lead form, use qualification questions that signal the offer is tailored, not generic (see 07-forms.md — qualification forms with 5-8 fields are appropriate here).

# Structure Adjustments

Relative to the default architecture (02), high-ticket pages typically benefit from:

- A stronger authority/credibility section earlier than usual
- An explicit process/methodology section
- Deeper case studies instead of short testimonial snippets
- Explicit handling of price-related objections (justify value before or alongside revealing investment level)
- A qualification-style form or a "book a call" CTA rather than an instant checkout

---

# Copy Tone

- Confident, calm, and restrained rather than hype-driven or urgent
- Precise language demonstrating expertise
- Avoid excessive exclamation points, aggressive urgency tactics, or "salesy" language — high-ticket buyers are skeptical of hard-sell tactics

---

# CTA Strategy

The primary CTA is often a consultation, application, or discovery call rather than immediate purchase, since high-ticket decisions typically require a conversation.

See 05-cta.md — CTA copy should reflect this next step honestly ("Book a strategy call", "Apply for a consultation").

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Using low-ticket tactics (aggressive urgency, discount countdowns) that undermine premium positioning
- Hiding the process, making the offer feel opaque or risky
- Generic testimonials instead of detailed case studies
- Asking for a purchase decision with too little information provided
- Competing on price instead of value

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Establish authority and expertise prominently.
2. Include a clear process/methodology section.
3. Use detailed case studies over short testimonials.
4. Frame price around value and outcome, not comparison discounting.
5. Use a qualification-oriented CTA (call, application) matched to the buying process.

---

# Quality Checklist

□ Authority/expertise clearly established

□ Process/methodology explained

□ Detailed case studies present

□ Price objection addressed through value framing

□ CTA matches the real next step in a high-ticket sales process

□ Tone is confident and restrained, not hype-driven

---

# AI Instructions

Before writing copy, understand the actual sales process for this offer (is it self-serve, or does it require a call/consultation?). The CTA and structure must reflect the real buying journey, not a generic ecommerce pattern.
