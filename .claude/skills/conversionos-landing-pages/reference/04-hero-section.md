# Purpose

This skill defines how to design and write the hero section — the first thing a visitor sees.

The hero determines whether the visitor continues or leaves. It carries disproportionate weight in conversion outcomes.

---

# Scope

Applies to the top section of any landing page, above the fold.

# Purpose of the Hero

Within seconds, the hero must answer:

1. What is this?
2. Is this for me?
3. What should I do next?

If any of these three is unclear, the visitor leaves.

---

# Hero Anatomy

## Headline

The main promise or identification. See 03 for frameworks.

---

## Subheadline

Supporting context: how it works, who it's for, or what makes it different.

---

## Primary CTA

The single most important action. Must be visually dominant.

---

## Secondary CTA (optional)

Lower-commitment action for visitors not ready to convert (e.g., "See how it works").

Only include if it does not compete visually with the primary CTA.

---

## Visual Element

An image, product shot, or illustration that reinforces the message.

Must answer: "What am I looking at, and why does it matter?"

Avoid decorative visuals with no informational value.

---

## Trust Indicator (optional)

A short proof element: client logos, a number, a rating.

Use when the audience needs immediate credibility before continuing.

---

# Hero Layout Patterns

## Split Layout

Text on one side, visual on the other. Best for products, services with a visual component.

---

## Centered Layout

Text-centered, visual below or as background. Best for strong, simple promises.

---

## Full-Bleed Visual

Large image or video as background with text overlay. Requires strong contrast handling for legibility.

---

# Hero Rules

- The primary CTA must be visible without scrolling on both desktop and mobile
- Avoid carousels/sliders in the hero — they dilute focus and hurt performance
- Do not put more than one primary message in the hero
- The visual must be relevant to the specific business, not generic stock imagery
- Load the hero visual with performance in mind (see 10)

---

# Matching Hero to Traffic Source

## Meta Ads (cold traffic)

Hero should identify the audience and problem clearly, since visitors may not know the brand.

---

## Google Ads (high intent)

Hero should mirror the search intent and confirm relevance immediately.

---

## Direct / Branded Traffic

Hero can assume familiarity and focus more on the specific offer.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Vague headlines that could apply to any business
- Multiple competing CTAs
- Hero visuals that don't relate to the offer
- Long paragraphs instead of a headline + subheadline
- Auto-playing video with sound

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Determine the single most important message for the hero.
2. Write headline and subheadline following 03.
3. Choose one primary CTA with clear, specific copy.
4. Select a visual that reinforces the message, not just decorates it.
5. Verify the CTA is visible above the fold on mobile.

---

# Quality Checklist

□ Answers "what is this" immediately

□ Answers "is this for me"

□ One clear primary CTA

□ Visual reinforces the message

□ Visible without scrolling on mobile

□ No competing messages

---

# AI Instructions

Treat the hero as the highest-leverage section on the page.

Spend proportionally more iteration on hero copy and layout than on any other single section.
