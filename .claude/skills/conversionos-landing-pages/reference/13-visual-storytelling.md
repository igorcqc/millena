# ConversionOS - Visual Storytelling Skill

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

This skill defines how to use imagery, layout, and sequencing to tell a coherent visual story that reinforces the page's message and builds emotional connection.

Visuals should communicate, not just decorate.

---

# Scope

Applies to photography, illustration, iconography, and the visual sequence across sections.

# Core Principle

Every image on the page should answer: "Why does this image help the visitor understand or trust the offer?"

If an image is purely decorative and doesn't reinforce the message, reconsider its inclusion.

---

# Visual Narrative Arc

A landing page can mirror a story structure through its visuals:

1. Identification — show the audience or context (who this is for)
2. Problem — show the pain point or before-state, when relevant
3. Solution/Product — show the offer clearly and specifically
4. Transformation — show the outcome or after-state
5. Proof — show real people, real results

Not every page needs all five stages visually, but the sequence should feel intentional, not random.

---

# Image Selection Rules

- Prioritize real photography of the actual product, service, team, or environment over generic stock imagery
- When stock imagery is unavoidable, choose images specific and authentic to the context, not generic "smiling business people" clichés
- Ensure represented people match the realistic target audience when relevant (age, context, setting)

---

# Product/Service Visualization

- Show the actual product from angles that answer real buyer questions (detail, scale, context of use)
- For services, visualize the process or outcome rather than abstract concepts
- For high-ticket or complex offers, consider before/after or process visuals that build understanding

---

# Consistency Rules

- Maintain a consistent visual tone across all images (lighting, color grading, style)
- Avoid mixing illustration styles with photography unless deliberately part of the brand system
- Icons should follow 00-design-system.md rules (same family, stroke, sizing)

---

# Emotional Tone Matching

Match imagery tone to the offer and audience:

- Premium/high-ticket: refined, uncluttered, higher production value
- Local business: authentic, approachable, real locations/people
- SaaS/tech: clean, functional, product-forward (UI screenshots, dashboards)

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Generic stock photos disconnected from the actual business
- Inconsistent visual style between sections
- Images that require a caption to make sense (they should be self-explanatory in context)
- Overusing illustration/abstract graphics when real product/service visuals would build more trust
- Visual clutter that competes with the copy for attention

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Map the visual narrative arc to the page's section structure.
2. Prioritize real, specific imagery over generic stock photography.
3. Maintain visual style consistency across the page.
4. Match emotional tone to audience and offer type.
5. Flag to the user when real photography/assets are needed and not yet available.

---

# Quality Checklist

□ Every image has a clear communicative purpose

□ Visual style is consistent throughout

□ Imagery matches the audience and offer tone

□ Real assets used or clearly flagged as placeholders

□ No generic, disconnected stock imagery

---

# AI Instructions

When real photography is not available, use clearly marked placeholders and tell the user what kind of asset is needed (e.g., "[Photo of the actual storefront/team needed here]") rather than filling gaps with generic stock images that undermine trust.
