# Purpose

This skill defines the full set of trust signals a landing page can use beyond social proof: transparency, security, professionalism, and risk reversal.

Trust is the deciding factor whenever the visitor is uncertain, regardless of how strong the offer is.

---

# Scope

Applies to any element that reduces perceived risk: guarantees, transparency, contact information, security signals, and professionalism cues.

# Core Principle

Trust is built by reducing uncertainty, not by claiming credibility.

Telling visitors "we're trustworthy" convinces no one. Showing transparency, proof, and professionalism does.

---

# Categories of Trust Signals

## Social Proof

See 06-social-proof.md — testimonials, reviews, numbers, logos.

---

## Transparency

- Clear, specific pricing or pricing logic (or a clear explanation of why pricing is custom)
- Honest description of what's included and excluded
- Visible contact information (phone, address for local businesses, email)
- Real team/company information, not anonymous branding

---

## Risk Reversal

- Guarantees (money-back, satisfaction, results-based) when legitimate and legally sound
- Free trials, samples, or consultations that let visitors evaluate before committing
- Clear cancellation or return policies

Never state a guarantee the business cannot or will not honor.

---

## Professionalism Signals

- Polished, consistent design (see 00-design-system.md)
- No spelling/grammar errors
- Working links and functional forms
- Fast, reliable performance (see 10-performance.md)

Poor execution on these basics undermines trust regardless of copy quality.

---

## Authority Signals

- Certifications, licenses, years in business
- Industry affiliations or partnerships
- Media mentions or press features
- Team credentials, when relevant to the offer

---

## Security Signals

For any form collecting sensitive data:

- Explicit privacy statement near the form ("Your information is never shared")
- HTTPS (baseline requirement, not optional)
- Recognizable payment security badges for checkout flows

---

# Matching Trust Signals to Objection Type

| Objection | Trust Signal |
|---|---|
| "Is this legitimate?" | Address, real team, licenses |
| "Will this work for me?" | Case studies matching visitor's situation |
| "Is this worth the price?" | Transparent value breakdown, guarantee |
| "What if I'm not satisfied?" | Guarantee, return policy, trial |
| "Is my data safe?" | Privacy statement, security badges |

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Vague trust claims without evidence ("Trusted by thousands" with no proof)
- Guarantees the business can't actually honor
- Hiding pricing or contact information entirely
- Overloading the page with badges/logos that don't relate to the actual offer
- Neglecting basic execution quality (broken links, typos, slow load)

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Identify the specific objections trust signals need to address (see 01).
2. Select trust signal types matched to those objections.
3. Ensure every trust claim is real and verifiable.
4. Verify basic professionalism execution (no errors, working links, fast load).
5. Place trust signals near the point of hesitation, especially near CTAs and pricing.

---

# Quality Checklist

□ Trust signals match real objections

□ All claims are honest and verifiable

□ Contact/business information is visible

□ Guarantees, if used, are legitimate and clear

□ No execution errors undermining credibility

---

# AI Instructions

When a business lacks strong social proof or track record, do not fabricate it. Instead, lean on transparency, guarantees, and professionalism signals that don't require a history to be credible.
