# Purpose

This skill defines how to write persuasive, clear copy for landing pages: headlines, subheadlines, benefits, and CTAs.

Copy is the primary driver of conversion. Design supports the message; it does not replace it.

---

# Scope

Applies to all written content on conversion pages: headlines, body copy, buttons, microcopy, and form labels.

# Core Principle

Good copy is not clever. It is clear.

The visitor should never have to re-read a sentence to understand it.

Every sentence must either:

- Increase motivation
- Increase clarity
- Increase trust
- Reduce friction

If a sentence does none of these, cut it.

# Headline Frameworks

## Outcome-Focused

Structure: [Desired Outcome] + [Without the Pain Point / Timeframe / Method]

Example: "Get qualified leads every week without wasting your ad budget"

---

## Problem-Solution

Structure: [Problem] → [Solution]

Example: "Struggling to fill your calendar? We book qualified consultations for you."

---

## Specific Claim

Structure: [Specific, believable result] + [For whom]

Example: "Furniture stores that generate 30+ qualified leads per month"

---

## Direct Identification

Structure: [Who this is for] + [What they get]

Example: "Planned furniture for families who want quality without the wait"

---

# Headline Rules

- Lead with the outcome, not the feature
- Be specific instead of generic
- Avoid unverifiable superlatives ("the best", "number one") unless backed by proof
- Match the headline to the traffic source's expectation (see 15, 16)

---

# Subheadline

Purpose: Add necessary context the headline could not fit.

Should answer one of:

- How does it work?
- Who is it for?
- What makes it different?

Avoid repeating the headline in different words.

---

# Benefit Writing Framework

Use the Feature → Benefit → Outcome structure.

## Feature

What it is.

## Benefit

What it does for the user.

## Outcome

What changes in their life or business.

Example:

Feature: "Automated WhatsApp follow-up"

Benefit: "Every lead gets contacted within minutes"

Outcome: "You stop losing sales to slow response times"

Never present a feature without connecting it to an outcome.

---

# CTA Copy

CTA text must be specific about the action and the value received.

## Strong Examples

- "Request my free quote"
- "See pricing for my project"
- "Talk to a specialist on WhatsApp"

## Weak Examples

- "Click here"
- "Submit"
- "Learn more" (only acceptable for secondary, low-commitment actions)

CTA copy should use first person or direct address when it increases ownership ("Get my plan" vs "Get your plan" — test based on brand voice).

---

# Objection-Handling Copy

For every known objection, write a direct, honest response.

Structure: [Acknowledge] + [Reframe or Answer] + [Proof if available]

Example:

Objection: "This seems expensive."

Copy: "Planned furniture is an investment that lasts 15+ years — see how our pricing compares to buying disposable furniture twice."

---

# Tone and Voice

Tone must match:

- The audience's sophistication level
- The price point (premium offers use more restrained, confident language; low-ticket offers can use more urgency and enthusiasm)
- The brand, if one already exists

Avoid generic marketing language: "innovative", "cutting-edge", "world-class" — these carry no information.

---

# Readability Rules

- Short sentences over long ones
- Common words over jargon, unless the audience expects technical language
- Active voice over passive voice
- One idea per sentence
- Break up text with subheadings, not walls of paragraphs

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Leading with the company instead of the visitor's problem
- Listing features without outcomes
- Vague claims without numbers or specifics
- Copy that could apply to any competitor
- CTAs that don't state the value of clicking

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Identify the core promise before writing any headline.
2. Write benefits using Feature → Benefit → Outcome.
3. Write CTA copy specific to the action and value.
4. Address the top 3-5 objections explicitly somewhere on the page.
5. Match tone to audience and price point.

---

# Quality Checklist

## Headline

□ Leads with outcome or clear identification

□ Specific, not generic

□ Matches traffic source expectation

## Body Copy

□ Feature-Benefit-Outcome used consistently

□ No jargon without purpose

□ Short, scannable sentences

## CTA

□ States the action clearly

□ Communicates value

## Objections

□ Top objections addressed directly

---

# AI Instructions

Before writing copy, list the visitor's problem, desired outcome, and top objections.

Write the headline last, after the argument of the page is clear.

Never generate copy that could be reused for a different business without changes — specificity is proof of quality.
