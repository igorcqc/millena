Version: 1.0

# Purpose

This skill defines how to design landing pages for software products and digital platforms, where the goal is typically trial signup, demo request, or subscription.

SaaS pages must communicate abstract product value quickly and lower the barrier to trying the product.

---

# Scope

Applies to software products, platforms, and digital tools sold via subscription or usage-based models.

# SaaS Visitor Psychology

SaaS visitors ask, beyond standard questions:

- "What does this actually do, concretely?"
- "How is this different from [known competitor/alternative]?"
- "How hard is this to set up and use?"
- "What does it cost as I scale?"
- "Can I try before committing?"

---

# Priority Elements

## Concrete Product Demonstration

Abstract claims ("streamline your workflow") convert worse than concrete demonstration: product screenshots, short demo videos, or interactive previews showing the actual interface and outcome.

---

## Outcome-Focused Headlines

Lead with the outcome the software enables, not the feature list (see 03 Feature → Benefit → Outcome framework), then support with specifics.

---

## Low-Friction Trial Path

Whenever possible, prioritize "Start free trial" or "Try it free" over "Contact sales" for product-led growth models — this removes the biggest barrier for self-serve SaaS.

For sales-led/enterprise SaaS, "Book a demo" is the appropriate primary CTA instead.

---

## Competitive Differentiation

SaaS buyers often already use or know an alternative (a competitor, or a manual process/spreadsheet). Address "why this instead of X" directly, even if implicitly.

---

## Pricing Clarity

Whenever pricing is public, present it clearly and simply. Hidden or confusing pricing is a common SaaS conversion killer. If pricing is usage-based or custom, explain the logic clearly.

---

# Structure Adjustments

Relative to the default architecture (02), SaaS pages typically benefit from:

- Product screenshot or demo directly in or near the hero
- A "how it works" section showing the workflow in 3-4 steps
- Integration/compatibility information if relevant (works with tools the audience already uses)
- Pricing section with clear tiers, or a clear CTA to see pricing
- Security/compliance signals for B2B or data-sensitive products

---

# Onboarding Continuity

The landing page CTA should connect smoothly into the actual product onboarding experience — the promise made on the page should be reflected immediately in the signup/trial flow.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Abstract, jargon-heavy copy with no concrete demonstration of the product
- Hiding the product behind a "Book a demo" wall for self-serve-appropriate products
- Confusing or hidden pricing
- Feature lists with no outcome framing
- Ignoring the competitive/alternative context the visitor is comparing against

---

# Implementation Rules

The AI must:

1. Include concrete product visuals (screenshots, demo) early on the page.
2. Choose the correct primary CTA model (self-serve trial vs. sales-led demo) based on the business model.
3. Frame features through outcomes, not just capability lists.
4. Present pricing clearly when public.
5. Address competitive differentiation explicitly.

---

# Quality Checklist

□ Concrete product demonstration present

□ CTA matches the business's actual sales model (self-serve vs. sales-led)

□ Features framed as outcomes

□ Pricing clear, or a clear path to see it

□ Competitive differentiation addressed

---

# AI Instructions

Before building the page, determine whether the product is self-serve (product-led growth) or requires a sales conversation (sales-led), since this changes the primary CTA and page structure significantly.
